**The flash:** use the camera’s mechanical sound and a short supernatural interruption. Do not substitute an explosion.

**The five-second freeze:** reduce the music and let stillness become uncomfortable. The birthday guest’s voice and movement are clear.

**The bite:** combine a brief impact, fabric movement, interrupted speech, and a short cry. Avoid exaggerated gore audio.

**The intercom:** use controlled speaker coloration. The voice must remain intelligible.

**The hunt:** emphasize footfalls, door movement, breathing, cloth, and occasional structural sounds. Silence should have value.

**The final minute:** add a restrained musical pulse and audible lockdown machinery without burying gameplay cues.

**Escape:** the exterior sound opens up. Let breathing settle. Do not immediately cancel relief with an unavoidable final attack.

The sound listener should represent the character’s physical location, not float high above the mansion with the travel camera.

Account for distance, doors, and intervening walls. Do not make nearby rooms sound identical to the player’s own room.

A soundtrack stinger must not reveal an unseen zombie through a wall. Threat music should follow events the player legitimately perceives.

Do not force automatic character whispers that betray a hidden player when they chose to stay quiet.

## 27. Handle audio, captions, and the real venue correctly

Support headphones, phone-speaker play, and a shared venue audio mode.

In shared venue mode, route common introduction and intercom audio through the designated host output to avoid twelve delayed copies of the same speech.

Keep private SOS content and secret role information off shared speakers.

Do not assume sound can start automatically. Use a clear player interaction to enable audio and handle blocked playback gracefully; browsers can restrict audible autoplay and Web Audio until appropriate interaction occurs. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="8"}

Provide master, music, effects, and voice controls.

Muting playback changes what the player hears, not whether their character’s running creates a gameplay sound event.

Caption dialogue accurately. For unseen nonverbal events, use descriptions such as **footsteps nearby**, not a secret identity or infection label.

Keep contrast between whispers and panic without unsafe output peaks. Use a limiter and avoid abrupt maximum-volume scares.

Participants remain together in the real venue. Their characters do the running and hiding inside the virtual mansion.

Do not require real running, crawling beneath venue furniture, microphone access, or unsafe movement.

## 28. Build the browser interface from joining through play

The participant journey remains:

**Scan QR code → open browser → enter the intended session → select an available character → get ready → play.**

No app installation, developer account, or complicated registration.

The opening screen should show a restrained nighttime mansion view, the game title, and the join action. Avoid decorative buttons that do nothing.

Character selection shows the existing identity, a readable portrait or model preview, availability, and a clear confirmation.

The host sees actual readiness and asset-loading status. Do not report ready while essential character or room assets are missing.

Freeze the cast and introduction assignments at match start.

Support portrait and landscape phone layouts. Landscape may offer more scene width, but do not make forced rotation a requirement for playing.

Keep touch targets generous, text readable, and controls clear of device safe areas.

During travel, emphasize destination, pace, Stop, and contextual actions.

Near a corner, show Peek. Near the portrait wall, show View Gallery. While hidden, show appropriate looking, peeking, leaving, and reachable interactions.

Keep the countdown visible without covering the scene.

Room selection uses recognizable static images captured from the actual rooms. Thumbnails must not expose current occupants.

Do not stack menus on top of each other. Closing a panel must not resume an old movement command unexpectedly.

Provide clear pending, accepted, blocked, reconnecting, and session-ended feedback.

## 29. Make the ending reflect actual outcomes

At zero, the mansion completes its final sealing.

Remaining unescaped survivors fail. Hiding does not prevent the deadline.

Use shutters, fading light, the final bell, and the collection’s imagery. Avoid an unrelated explosion.

The portrait wall should make the consequence understandable.

Current guest portraits may appear in the ending only after secret information is no longer relevant.

Escaped players retain their success. Do not add a surprise curse or forced death that invalidates it.

The ending reflects who escaped, who helped, who turned, and who remained.

A larger group escape can show a warmer reunion. A lone escape can be quieter. Neither should falsify the outcome.

Preserve the birthday guest’s established fate. Do not invent a cure in the ending.

If all original survivors reach resolved outcomes before the deadline, end active play. A later final-lockdown epilogue must be clearly presented as a time jump rather than secretly changing the timer rule.

Show the infection timeline only after the match.

A final glimpse of another prepared invitation may suggest Elias remains a threat. It must not cancel the current group’s win or require a sequel.

## 30. Preserve fair multiplayer and private information

The server validates movement, visibility permissions, searches, grabs, bites, infection, blocking, defenses, camera ownership, cooldowns, rescue, escape, and match time.

Clients send requested actions and render permitted outcomes.

Maintain one shared match containing the mansion’s connected locations. Do not create a separate disconnected multiplayer session for each gallery room.

Protect hidden state in transmitted data, not merely in interface labels. Colyseus State Views support per-client filtering; use compatible filtering and private messages rather than broadcasting every secret to every player. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="9"}

Visible encounters require enough data to render the encountered character. They do not justify distributing a global infection directory.

Bots use permitted perception and remembered evidence.

Shared displays and spectators must not expose hidden positions or secret transformations to active participants.

Reconnection restores the same eligible identity and legitimate state. It cannot duplicate characters, reset infection, restart the timer, or create another camera.

Interrupted inspections and camera transitions must recover cleanly.

A disconnected renderer must not reset the match.

For last-second escapes, use one documented server-side ordering rule. Crossing must complete before the deadline.

## 31. Make the visuals achievable in the actual browser

The target is a polished, visually convincing browser game—not a promise that every phone can render unlimited detail.

Use real rigged character assets, properly authored materials, optimized geometry, and suitable animation clips.

Preserve the standalone engine’s current build pipeline. Verify package compatibility before changing APIs.

Use a tested rendering path with appropriate fallback support. Do not require a graphics feature merely because it is available on the development computer.

Profile the full scene rather than optimizing an empty room. PlayCanvas’s guidance emphasizes controlling draw calls, batching appropriate objects, and avoiding unnecessary per-frame allocations. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="10"}

Use compressed textures through a compatible asset pipeline. Small image download size alone does not establish low texture-memory use; PlayCanvas documents that distinction and supports compressed texture workflows. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="11"}

Initial production targets are sustained thirty frames per second on the chosen supported phone baseline and sixty on a suitable desktop test configuration. These are targets to measure, not claims already achieved.

Test a full fifteen-minute run for heat, memory growth, frame pacing, and connection stability.

Reduce decorative particles, reflection detail, distant geometry, and shadow cost before sacrificing essential character recognition, controls, or visibility rules.

Do not load every high-resolution portrait and every voice variation before the player can join.

Preload essential gameplay geometry, collision, core animations, and required opening assets. Stream optional detail safely.

## 32. Maintain an honest asset-production manifest

For each room, character, animation, sound, portrait, and cinematic component, record whether it is existing and usable, needs refinement, newly produced, or still a placeholder.

Track source, usage permission, local path, runtime format, optimization status, and the feature that consumes it.

Use available authorized production tools. Do not claim that unavailable modeling, animation, or generation tools were used.

Do not extract copyrighted models, textures, music, or voices from the reference images or unrelated commercial games.

Do not purchase assets or hire performers without approval.

Preserve source assets and optimized runtime exports separately.

When a required asset is missing, identify the exact missing deliverable. Do not quietly replace a detailed character with a capsule and mark the feature complete.

A temporary substitute may support testing, but it must remain clearly labeled in the handoff.

## 33. Build in integrated stages without stopping at a plan

First, verify the working baseline and asset inventory.

Next, integrate View Gallery and prove that inspection remains part of the same live match.

Then apply the interaction system consistently across the existing rooms.

Integrate the dynamic-cast opening, camera continuity, intercom, timer, and ending.

Perform the visual, animation, and audio refinement on those working systems.

Do not spend the entire task creating cinematic shots while the player’s movement remains broken.

Do not stop after producing a project plan when implementation can continue.

Commit coherent checkpoints and keep the documentation current. A saved checkpoint is not the same as a finished deployment.

Provide the updated playable URL through the existing authorized deployment process.

Identify the deployed frontend and backend versions so the link can be matched to the tested build.

The final quality standard is this:

**The invitation gets them inside.**

**The birthday makes the gathering personal.**

**The photograph reveals that Elias can be resisted.**

**The bite turns familiar faces into uncertainty.**

**The gallery reveals what failure means.**

**The deadline makes permanent hiding impossible.**

**The controls let players create their own escape story.**

**Their decisions determine who leaves.**

The player should remember something that genuinely happened:

“I stopped to look at the portraits. Then I heard footsteps. I recognized the shoes, so I almost came out. But he bent down, and I saw his face. My friend flashed the camera. We ran through the hallway together. We had forty seconds left.”

**Build the systems, performances, and environment that make that moment possible. Do not replace it with a cutscene that tells the player it happened.**
